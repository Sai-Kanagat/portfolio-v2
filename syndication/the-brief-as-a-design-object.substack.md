# The Brief as a Design Object

I have read a lot of creative briefs. Most of them are not briefs — they are summaries. They describe what the brand does, what the campaign is for, who the audience is (vaguely), and what success looks like (even more vaguely). Then they ask for creative work.

This is not a brief. This is a prompt. And prompts produce generic responses.

## What a brief is for

A brief is a design object. Its function is to constrain creative thinking in exactly the right places so that the energy which would otherwise be spent on "what should this be about" gets redirected into "how do we do this brilliantly."

The best industrial design briefs I ever worked with were almost absurdly specific. Every constraint was a design decision already made. Our job was to work within them with imagination.

**Specificity is not a constraint on creativity. It is the engine of it.**

## The four things a brief must do

**1. Name the real problem.** "We want more Instagram followers" is not a problem. "Our brand is perceived as premium but our conversion rate suggests our audience doesn't trust us enough to pay premium prices" is.

**2. Define the single person you're talking to.** Not a demographic. A person. What does she already believe? What does she need to believe? What bridges the two?

**3. State the one thing the work must communicate.** Not three. One. If the audience remembers nothing else, what must they remember?

**4. Define success in a way that can be measured.** Not "increase brand awareness." What specific behaviour change, and how will you know?

## The brief as a contract

When a piece of work is reviewed and someone says "I don't like the colour," the brief is how you redirect: "Does the colour serve the brief?" Most creative disagreements are actually brief disagreements.

> The brief is not the output. The brief is the design that makes the output possible.

## The one-sentence test

Can the entire brief be distilled into a single sentence a creative team could brief themselves from? "Make a 35-year-old Italian woman who already buys organic feel like this brand understands her better than she understands herself."

If I can write that sentence, the brief is ready. If I can't, I haven't found the real problem yet.
